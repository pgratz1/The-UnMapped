# P1-01 elevation, design candidate v13: critic's review

2026-11-15 · world critic · candidate `p1/elevation-explore` commit 3396de5

Material reviewed: `~/.cache/the-unmapped/out/p1-elevation-review/explore/` (called `explore/` below). I looked at `v13-{512,1024,2048}-contact.png`, every per-seed image under `v13/1024/` and `v13/2048/`, the 512 seeds where the thumbnail was too small to judge (`v13/512/s10.png`, `s13.png`), the four 1:1 crops, the three rivers-and-lakes overlays, `v12-vs-v13-1024-contact.png`, the rejected original `../contact-512-seeds-0-19.png`, and the three stats and per-seed files. I also measured slopes, local relief and land extent directly from the `v13/1024/*.bin` and `v13/2048/*.bin` grids (int16, decimetres). The scripts are throwaway and not in the repository. Pixel coordinates below are in map cells of the named image, with (0, 0) at the north-west corner.

## 1. Verdict

**Another round, narrowly scoped: keep v13's continent outline and belt layout, rework the relief inside them.** The outlines are a real advance over the rejected terrain and over v12, and they can be frozen. The relief cannot go into a spec yet. Four checklist items fail outright: valleys (5), lakes (7, partly), artefacts (8) and history's places (10, partly). The mountains are far too steep to walk: the median slope of land above 500 m is 45° to 60° in every 1024 and 2048 seed. About a quarter of the seeds are mountain-light worlds that do not give the design its dwarves, its borders or its passes. Writing the revised spec now would turn these defects into requirements, and the proposed statistics would then approve them.

The fixes are tuning and algorithm changes inside the stage. None needs a design change. Section 6 lists them in order of priority.

## 2. What works

- **Outlines.** These are continents, not discs. 1024/2048 s0 has a deep northern inlet, an offshore island chain and a long south-eastern peninsula. 2048 s2 has an enclosed gulf with a narrow mouth, and 512 s10 a lagoon ringed by a broken arc of land. Compared with `../contact-512-seeds-0-19.png`, where every seed is the same round island, this is the step the rework needed. The v12 rounded-square edge (`v12-vs-v13-1024-contact.png`, top row: seeds 1, 4, 9) is mostly gone.
- **Range layout.** Most seeds now have one or two belts with a direction. 1024 s8 has two parallel ranges with a gap between them, and 2048 s2 has a northern range, a southern range and a central massif joining them. The worm-shaped walls of the original are much reduced.
- **Big-picture variety.** About half the seeds read as different places. Section 4 covers the rest.
- **Lakes in mountains.** 2048 s0 and s2 (`v13-2048-s0-rivers-lakes-half.png`, `v13-2048-s2-rivers-lakes.png`) carry tarn-like lakes along the range crests, which is what alpine country should have.
- **Large-scale statistics are under control.** `land_fraction` is 0.40 to 0.41 everywhere, and `largest_share` is at least 0.977. `step_p999` at 1024 and 2048 is 50 to 71 m.

## 4. Across seeds

**Memorable:** 1024/2048 s0 (inlet, islands, Y-shaped massif), 2048 s2 (gulf, two ranges, central knot), 1024 s8 (twin ranges and a gap), 512 s2 (long east-west massif with a ragged northern archipelago) and 512 s10 (broken ring around a lagoon).

**Dull:** s1 and 2048 s1 (about 95% one green plain, with p90 at 111.6 m at 2048), s4, s9, 512 s12 and 512 s13. That is roughly a quarter of the seeds. Section 6 D4 argues these should not ship.

**Recurring tells**, visible as soon as the seeds are laid side by side:

1. **Same place, same size.** In all 13 measured grids the land centroid lies within 0.07 of the map centre, and the land spans 0.75 to 0.91 of the map side on both axes. Every continent is a roughly circular blob of the same size in the same place, with its bays cut into it. A hand-drawn map would sometimes push land against one side, or split it with a sea.
2. **The circle shows through.** The outer sea shows a faint circular shelf at roughly 0.42 of the map side from the centre. It is clearest in 1024 s4 (west and north), 1024 s9 (west) and 2048 s1. Several coasts follow that arc rather than their own shape: the east coast of the 1024 s0 north-east lobe and the 2048 s2 east coast.
3. **One belt, often an arc that wraps a plain.** In seven of ten 1024 seeds a single belt or massif dominates. In s3, s5, s6 and s7 it is a crescent around a bowl-shaped plain, and 1024 s7 reads as a crater. This is a softened form of the rejected worm wall: constant width, smooth flanks, knobbed crest (1024 s5 is the clearest example).
4. **Mountains on the shore.** Belts or walls stand directly on the coast in 1024 s4 (west), 1024 s9 (west), 1024 s1 (south-east), 1024 s7 (north-east), and at 512 in s4, s9, s10, s12 and s13. Coastal ranges exist in reality, but here they are the commonest placement, and at 512 they leave a central plain fringed by mountains.
5. **One lowland texture.** Every lowland in every seed has the same fine, even bumpiness (median 1 km relief 38 to 64 m in most seeds, 86 to 98 m in 1024 s0 and s2), with no plateaus, scarps, downs, broad valleys or hill country of a different grain. At the scale of a contact sheet the worlds differ. At the scale of a crop, any lowland could be swapped for any other.

## 5. Map sizes

- **512 (8 km).** It reads as a small island and fits the purpose. But it uses the same 2000 to 2500 m peaks on a belt capped at 0.15 of the map side (about 1.2 km half-width), so it is far steeper than the others. `max_step` reaches 290.5 m (s13) and `step_p999` 205 m (s10). In s10 3.7% of land, and in s11, s13 and s15 more than 2%, has a cell-to-cell step over 80 m, which is a 79° slope. In 13 of 20 seeds `coast_ratio` is under 2.0. In seeds 9, 10, 12 and 13 the mountains are a rim around a plain (`v13/512/s13.png`: a single knife ridge along the south-east coast). 512 needs lower peaks; the engineer's S1 and S2 sqrt scaling was the right idea, rejected too early.
- **1024 (16 km).** This is the best balanced size, and the source of most of the findings above.
- **2048 (32 km).** It reads as a large land, at the same grain of detail as 1024. 2048 s2 is the best world in the set. The size magnifies both extremes. A mountain-light world becomes 800 km² of identical bumpy plain (2048 s1, where `above500` is 0.028). The fill plates grow to several km² (2048 s2: the inland basin at x ≈ 800 to 1050, y ≈ 990 to 1170, and the east plain at x ≈ 1570 to 1800, y ≈ 530 to 740; 2048 s1: two plates in the north-east). Only 3 seeds were shown, and 13.2 asks for 5.

## 6. Fitness for the game and for history

- **Walkability.** This is the most serious finding, and the images hide it. Measured from the grids, land above 500 m has a median slope of 45° to 60° at 1024 and 2048, and 69% to 92% of it is steeper than 35°. Taking all land, 3% to 22% is steeper than 45° (8% or more in every 1024 seed; 1024 s2: 22%). Real mountains measured on a 10 to 30 m grid average roughly 25° to 35°, and slopes over 45° are crags, not whole mountainsides. A first-person player cannot walk up 45°. As generated, every range is a solid wall broken only where a valley happens to cross it, and the flanks have no valleys (item 3). Dwarven holds need approaches, passes need saddles, and alpine biomes need ground that can be stood on. The cause is in the statistics: `p100` of 2000 to 2500 m on belts only a few km wide. Lower peaks (1200 to 1800 m), wider belts, or both, are needed. Steepness is a terrain-wide problem, not only a 512 one.
- **Lowlands at walking scale.** The opposite defect: hummocky everywhere, flat nowhere. A 3° to 9° bump field with 30 to 60 m of relief per km is tiring and featureless to walk through. It offers no river terraces, floodplains or marsh flats, and no prominent knolls for a castle. A real lowland is mostly flat ground with a few distinct hills and incised valleys. It is not uniform noise.
- **Six races on one continent.** In the good seeds (s0, s2, s3, s6, s7, s8, 2048 s0, 2048 s2) the ranges divide the land into three to five regions with natural borders. That gives elven heartlands on the larger plain, dwarven mountains, goblins and orcs in the poorer country beyond the range, and contested passes between. In s1, s4, s9 and 512 s12 and s13 the land is one undivided plain plus a mountain knob. History would have no borders to fight over except lines it invents, and the dwarves would be crowded onto about 5 km² (1024 s1, 2048 s1).
- **Wild places for faeries, hidden sites, reasons to explore.** Hiding needs broken ground: gorges, wooded hill country, marsh, side valleys. v13 has two textures, wall and bump field, and neither hides anything. Islands and peninsulas (s0, s2, 512 s10) are the only natural refuges, and they are good ones.
- **Defensible sites.** These exist on peninsulas and at belt ends: 1024 s5's eastern hook peninsula and 1024 s0's northern peninsula. They are rare inland, because there are no spurs and no isolated hills.

## 7. Defects, by priority

Seeds are 1024 unless noted.

**Blockers (must be fixed before the spec is revised)**

- **D1 Unwalkably steep mountains.** Median slope of land above 500 m is 45° to 60° in all 13 measured worlds, with 69% to 92% steeper than 35°. At 512 `frac_step80` reaches 3.7% (s10). Fix: lower `p100` toward 1500 to 1800 m at 1024 and 2048, scale peak height with belt width at 512 (as the sqrt scaling S1 and S2 did), and widen belts. Re-measure with the slope statistics proposed in section 8.
- **D2 Polygonal fill plates with parallel straight channels.** These are the engineer's "parallel straight channels on part-filled plains", and they are worse than "remaining defect" suggests. They are dead-flat plates of up to several km² with straight or right-angled outlines and 45° parallel channels: `v13-2048-s2-mountain-1to1.png` (x ≈ 0 to 150, y ≈ 90 to 300, with a straight edge at y ≈ 270), `v13-2048-s0-coast-lowland-1to1.png`, 1024 s1 (x ≈ 630 to 830, y ≈ 300 to 420), 1024 s7 (x ≈ 460 to 580, y ≈ 200 to 290), 1024 s9 (x ≈ 550 to 600, y ≈ 760 to 830), and 2048 s1 and s2. A player will walk onto these. Fix: the fill surface must follow the basin's natural floor with noise, or the basin must be breached. A gradient flood-fill from the outlet always draws its distance contours.
- **D3 Axis-aligned striping on slopes.** Column-aligned gullies on north-facing slopes in s4 (x ≈ 650 to 900, y ≈ 480 to 520) and s7 (x ≈ 460 to 660, y ≈ 460 to 500), and row-aligned streaks on s5's west flank. The overall gradient-direction histogram is uniform: 4.1% to 4.8% of slope cells lie within 2° of an axis, against 4.4% expected. So the striping is local, on smooth planar flanks where single-direction (D8) erosion cuts straight down a grid axis. Fix: break up the flank before erosion (relief noise on the aprons, which also helps D5), or use multiple-direction erosion on slopes.
- **D4 Mountain-light worlds.** 1024 s1 and s9, 2048 s1, and 512 s9, s10, s12 and s13: about a quarter of seeds. `above500` is below 0.08 (2048 s1: 0.028) and the high ground is one round massif. They fail checklist items 3 and 10 and the dwarves' and borderlands' needs. Fix: a floor on belt coverage in the segmentation, such as at least two belt segments, or at least 10% of land above 500 m, so that no seed is a plain with a knob. Do not widen the statistics to admit them (section 8).

**Should fix (before or during the spec revision)**

- **D5 Flanks without valleys; ranges as knobbed bands.** Smooth aprons on s5 (east flank), s8 and the 2048 s2 north range, with crests made of blobs. Item 3 needs spurs and side valleys reaching down the flanks. Part of the cure is D1 and D3.
- **D6 Uniform lowland texture, no flats, no lowland valleys.** Lowland median slope is 3.4° to 10°, coastal fringe 5° to 9°, and land flatter than 0.5° is 1% to 4%. Wanted: broad near-flat floodplains and coastal plains, distinct hill groups, and valleys that widen downstream instead of single-cell slots.
- **D7 Edge-aligned and falloff-shaped coasts.** s9 north and west, s1 east, s4 north-east. Coasts also follow the circle (s0 north-east lobe, 2048 s2 east), and the faint circular sea shelf appears in most seeds (s4, s9, 2048 s1). This is the engineer's "circular falloff" defect. It is visible in about half the seeds and drives the "always the same circular blob" tell. Fix: noisier and less circular falloff, and a seeded offset or elongation of the land mask, so the continent is not always centred and round.
- **D8 Too little coast detail at 100 m to 1 km.** `coast_ratio` is below the typical 2.0 in 6 of 10 seeds at 1024 and 13 of 20 at 512. Visually, smooth lobes join the big bays. Add coastal noise at metre scale, as a physical-relief quantity.
- **D9 Arcs and rims.** Crescents wrapping bowls (s3, s5, s6, s7), and belts on the shoreline as the commonest placement (section 4, tells 3 and 4). Steer belt placement away from the continent's edge band and limit belt curvature.

**Cosmetic (fix when convenient)**

- **D10 Rectilinear river segments in lowlands.** The engineer's item: short straight D8 runs in 1024 s1 (x ≈ 520 to 620, y ≈ 430 to 540) and parallel north-flowing streams on the s9 north coast. Most of this disappears once D2 and D6 are fixed. On its own it would not block.
- **D11 Wedge-shaped lake with a straight side** (s3, x ≈ 415 to 450, y ≈ 565 to 600), and seeds at 512 without lakes (4, 5, 6, 7, 15). Acceptable at 8 km if the hydrology statistics allow zero lakes at 512.

Status of the engineer's own list: the parallel channels on fill plates are visible, and I rate them a blocker (D2). The circular falloff is visible, should fix (D7). Rectilinear river segments are visible, cosmetic (D10). Mountain-light worlds are visible, a blocker (D4). Steepness at 512 is real, but it understates the problem: it is a blocker at every size (D1).

## 8. The statistics, and the proposed ranges

I could not find the engineer's proposed ranges in `INDEX.txt` or in any stats file. The `hard-bound breaks` columns are measured against bounds close to P1-00 13.1, and none of the files states a revised table. So I judge the proposal as the lead described it.

- **The measured ranges match the images.** `p90`, `above500` and `above1500` single out exactly the mountain-light seeds (512: 9, 12, 13; 1024: 9; 2048: 1). `step_p999` singles out the steep 512 seeds. `coast_ratio` misses the typical lower bound where the coasts look smooth. The statistics are doing their job.
- **Relaxing the elevation bounds to admit mountain-light worlds excuses a defect.** The bounds `p90` ≥ 300 m and `above500` ≥ 0.06 express "uplands exist", and the design needs that (dwarves, highland moor, borders). The worlds that break them are the dull ones in section 4. Keep the bounds and change the generator (D4). If anything, add a structural statistic: the length of the longest connected area above 500 m as a fraction of the map side, or the count of such areas over a minimum size.
- **Replacing `max_step` with `step_p999`: right idea, wrong bound, wrong measure.** One worst cell is a noisy statistic, so a high percentile is fairer. But a hard bound high enough to pass 512 s10 (205 m, an 85° step at one land cell in a thousand) approves cliffs. More importantly, neither statistic sees D1: every 1024 seed passes `step_p999` at 52 to 71 m, yet most of its mountain land is steeper than 45°. Add slope-distribution statistics: the share of land steeper than 35° and steeper than 45°, and the median slope of land above 500 m. Bound them from real terrain. My starting guess: under 15% of land steeper than 35°, under 3% steeper than 45°, and a median mountain slope of 25° to 35°. Keep `step_p999` ≤ 100 m as a hard bound at every size.
- **`coast_ratio` typical 2.0 to 4.5:** keep it. The misses are real (D8).
- **Lakes at 512:** allow zero lakes in the typical range at 512 (the /4 scaling already rounds the lower bound to 0). Keep `lake_share`'s hard lower bound for 1024 and 2048 only.
- **Add a lowland statistic:** the share of land below 200 m with slope under 1°, to catch D6. Floodplains and coastal plains need a real share, perhaps 0.05 to 0.25.

## 9. Views and data I wished for

1. A **slope map** (degrees, banded at 15°, 30°, 35° and 45°) for each 1024 seed. It would have shown D1 at once, where the hypsometric hillshade hides it.
2. **Hydrology statistics for v13**: `river_share`, `major_rivers`, `longest_river_m` and `largest_basin_share`, plus an overlay with river width by stream order and the lake outlets marked. Without them item 6 cannot be judged.
3. **20 seeds at 1024 and 5 at 2048**, as 13.2 requires. Seeds 10 to 19 at 1024 would also give ten continents not already seen at 512.
4. **1:1 crops of 1024 lowland and mountain** for at least three seeds (only 2048 crops exist for v13), and a **512 mountain crop** to see the 512 steepness.
5. A **ground-level or oblique render** (a few views per seed at eye height) to judge walkability and landmark quality. A top-down hillshade is the wrong instrument for a first-person game.
6. A **detrended lowland relief** image for v13, as was made for v8 to v11, to judge the bump field independently of the hypsometric tint.
